- [ ] **A focuser temperature reading must be GATED before it is trusted, and the archive proves why.** Prerequisite for the temperature-compensation item below. Many focusers take the probe on a separate port (the Gemini Focuser Pro is a rebadged myFocuserPro2 with a DS18B20 probe header), so a reading can be well-formed and still meaningless. Measured in the archive: **four Gemini Focuser Pro sessions report `FOCTEMP` = exactly `+20.00` for the entire run** (317, 273, 264 and 56 frames, **one distinct value**), which is a firmware placeholder with nothing plugged in, and one 2025-02-06 session reports `+31.13` frozen across 355 frames. <br>**Neither is caught by what exists.** `GeminiFocuserProtocol.GetTemperatureAsync` returns `double.NaN` when the reply is unparseable, but the firmware answers `:06#` with a well-formed `20.0`, so the NaN convention passes it; and `31.13` passes any plausibility range too. **`TempCompAvailable` is not this signal either**: it says the controller can compensate, not that it can currently sense. <br>**Probe presence is RUNTIME STATE, not a static capability**: a probe gets unplugged between sessions and a wire breaks mid-session, so this cannot be a connect-time flag. Three independent gates, because each catches a different failure: (1) no temperature capability at all -> `NotSupported`; (2) `NaN` or physically impossible -> `NoProbe` (a range of about -50 to +60 C catches the DS18B20's famous **+85.0** power-on-reset and **-127** bus-fault values, and ZWO's **-273.15**, with **no value blacklist needed**); (3) **liveness** -> `Frozen`, and this is the one that matters, since it is the only gate that catches both archive cases. <br>**Discriminate on DISTINCT VALUE COUNT, not on span.** A DS18B20 at 12-bit resolves 0.0625 C and real overnight cooling is 0.19 to 1.53 C/hour (measured, n=22), so a live probe always dithers across several values; every broken trace above has **exactly one**. A span threshold would wrongly condemn a genuinely stable night. <br>**The nastiest case this also catches**: a probe dangling in the warm electronics box rather than strapped to the tube reads *plausibly* and *changes*, but tracks the wrong body. Compare its rate against the archive envelope (roughly -0.2 to -1.5 C/hour while the night cools) and flag a near-zero rate over a long window. <br>Shape it as a pure evaluator returning a status plus a `Describe()` message, mirroring `DeviceOwnershipGate.Evaluate` and `FlatExposureSolver.Decide`; keep the recent-readings window in the existing lock-free `CircularBuffer<T>`. **Invariant: never compensate, and never trigger a refocus on temperature, unless the status is usable**, and fall back to the existing HFD-trend trigger, which needs no probe.
- [ ] **Temperature-driven refocus + open-loop focus compensation, with coefficients MEASURED from the D: archive (2026-08-04).** Today `IFocuserDriver` exposes `GetTemperatureAsync` and `Get/SetTempCompAsync`, but the latter is pure pass-through to the focuser's own firmware and **nothing in `Session` reads focuser temperature at all**. The only refocus trigger is `FocusDriftDetector.EstimateTrendHfd` against `FocusDriftThreshold` (1.07), which is reactive by construction: focus must already have degraded ~7% before it fires, and it needs `FocusDriftMinSamples` comparable frames to see the trend. <br>**Every N.I.N.A. frame in the archive records `FOCUSPOS` and `FOCTEMP`**, so the coefficient is already measured rather than guessed. Fitted over 33 sessions / 5,740 frames on the ASI533MC Pro at FL130 with the EAF, using three independent estimators that agree: **pooled fixed-effects fit 5.37, median per-session slope 5.65, travel-over-temperature-span 5.86 steps per C**. Overnight cooling rate, n=22 distinct sessions: median **-0.59 C/hour** (range -0.19 to -1.53). <br>**Both `FakeFocuserDriver` guesses are thereby validated**: `_tempCoefficient = 5.0` against 5.37 to 5.86 measured, and `_tempDriftRate = -0.5` C/hour against -0.59 measured (reality cools ~18% faster). Consider nudging the fake to the measured values, and note the test-plan comment at line 68 that assumes `2 C -> 10 steps` stays right either way. <br>**Two separable features.** (a) *Predictive trigger*: refocus when `|FOCTEMP - tempAtLastAutofocus|` crosses a threshold, ahead of the frames being spoiled instead of after. The threshold is empirical, not a guess: uncompensated SharpCap sessions held focus at `<= 1.1 C` and 6 of 7 degraded 6 to 10% above `1.4 C` (see the focus-triage section in [imaging.md](imaging.md)), which straddles the existing 7% HFD threshold, so **1.0 to 1.5 C** fires at about the same time the HFD trigger would. Combined with the coefficient this puts the **critical focus zone for that rig at 6 to 8 steps**, derived from measurement rather than from `2*lambda*N^2` plus a step size the header does not give (`FOCUSSZ` reads 60000, which is total travel, not step size). N.I.N.A.'s own observed travel of 14 to 26 steps per night is 2 to 4 CFZ widths, consistent with it refocusing two or three times. (b) *Open-loop compensation*: move by `stepsPerC * dT` between AutoFocus runs. <br>**Do this host-side, and the reason is a gap in the ASCOM interface rather than a preference.** `TempComp` / `TempCompAvailable` / `Temperature` / `StepSize` are `IFocuserV3`/`V4` properties, so the on/off switch is standard, but **ASCOM defines no temperature COEFFICIENT anywhere**. There is no `TempCompCoefficient`. The steps-per-degree lives either in the controller's EEPROM (myFocuserPro2 style, set from the handset or vendor app) or in the ASCOM driver's own setup dialog, so a host **cannot read it, set it, or audit what the firmware just did**. Firmware compensation is a black box with a toggle. Host-side it can be fitted from our own AutoFocus history, verified against the next AutoFocus, logged, and turned off per rig. **Hard invariant: call `SetTempCompAsync(false)` before compensating ourselves, or the firmware and the host both move the focuser and the compensation is applied twice.** <br>**And on how a focuser knows its step size: it does not, ever.** `StepSize` is always a user-supplied configuration value, never a measurement: Gemini's `:33#` returns an EEPROM value the user entered, ASCOM/Alpaca return a driver setup-dialog value, and the ZWO/QHY native drivers do not expose one at all (`CanGetStepSize => false`). It is a property of the mechanical coupling (helicoid pitch, Crayford reduction, what the motor actually drives) which the electronics have no way to observe. So **never treat `StepSize` as ground truth for a focus model**; work in steps, which is what the archive coefficient is already expressed in, and keep microns for display only. The archive shows the same trap on the FITS side: `FOCUSSZ` reads 60000 for the EAF, which is total travel in microns, not step size. <br>**Layering: temperature is the PRIMARY trigger, the HFD trend stays as the secondary backstop** (user, 2026-08-04). HFD always runs, needs no probe, and catches everything temperature cannot: a bumped focuser, a filter offset, tube flexure, dew. Temperature only buys earliness. <br>**Store the coefficient PER OTA, not per focuser, and in a sidecar rather than the profile JSON.** Per OTA because it is a property of the optical train (tube material, whether the motor drives a helicoid or a Crayford), which the archive demonstrates: the *same* QFocuser model gives an unusable spread on the SV605CC rig and a **negative** slope on the QHY294/SW8Q. In a sidecar because it is a *learned, refined* value with a sample count and a timestamp, exactly like backlash: mirror `Profiles/BacklashHistory/<focuserDeviceId>.json` as a per-OTA thermal history, and keep it out of the profile JSON, which holds static hardware facts (the same reason live pressure/temperature were deliberately excluded, see the site-conditions entry above). `OTAData` is where the identity comes from, not where the estimate lives. <br>**On training a model like the neural guider: the data supports ONE parameter, not twenty-six.** `NeuralGuideModel` is a 26 -> 32 -> 16 -> 2 MLP because guiding is a nonlinear dynamical system with seeing, periodic error, backlash and wind all interacting. Thermal focus drift over a night's 2 to 6 C is very nearly linear, and it was measured so: on the open-loop 2024-12-05 session (focuser frozen at 47477 for 2h17m) HFD tracks `FOCTEMP` at **-0.126 px/C** with a residual of 0.035 px. **The one part of the guider design that DOES transfer is `NeuralGuidePerformanceMonitor`**: it refuses to use the model unless it beats the P-controller baseline by 15% over a minimum sample count. A thermal model owes the same gate against the plain HFD-trend trigger, per OTA. <br>**A thermal LAG term is NOT yet justified, and the archive cannot settle it.** The tail of that session is suggestive (temperature plateaus after 13:57 while HFD keeps climbing), and a first-order lag fits 24% better than the instantaneous model at tau ~1.45 h. But `r(FOCTEMP, elapsed) = -0.953`, so temperature and time are collinear, and **a pure elapsed-time model fits better than either** (residual 0.0251 vs 0.0267 vs 0.0351). Anything monotonic explains this night. Settling it needs a dedicated experiment where temperature *reverses* direction, or a deliberate step, not more archive mining. **Ship the single slope first.** <br>**The airmass confound was checked and eliminated, and it strengthens the case.** On that session airmass went 1.522 -> **1.198** (altitude 41 -> 56.5 deg), so the target was rising and seeing improving, yet HFD still degraded 10.2%; the confound would have *masked* the drift, not caused it, so the real degradation is larger than measured. `r(airmass, FOCTEMP) = +0.996`, so the two are nearly collinear and only the sign identifies which is causal. Star count also fell 840 -> 745 *while altitude rose*, which is defocus spreading stars below the detection threshold rather than extinction. **Any future fit on archive data must include this check**, because on a setting target airmass and temperature move together and a naive fit would credit temperature with both. <br>**Three independent numbers agree, which is the main reason to trust any of this:** the coefficient (5.86 steps/C), the HFD sensitivity (-0.126 px/C), and the tolerance from the SharpCap group (1.1 to 1.4 C) compose to 0.14 to 0.18 px of HFD change on a 2.45 px baseline, i.e. **5.7 to 7.2%**, which lands on the existing `FocusDriftThreshold` of 7% without having been fitted to it. <br>The coefficient can be **self-validating the same way `BacklashEstimator` already is**: infer it from consecutive AutoFocus results (position + `FOCTEMP` at each) and EWMA it per focuser, persisted beside `Profiles/BacklashHistory/`. <br>**Four caveats.** The coefficient is **per rig, not per focuser** (it is a property of the whole optical train): the SV605CC/QFocuser rig's two estimators **disagree badly** (pooled 3.00 vs median 13.45 over 8 sessions), so it is not yet usable, and the QHY294 on the SW8Q gives a **negative** slope (-1.75) from a single session, which is plausible for a Newtonian but is n=1. A **per-session slope is not a thermal coefficient**, because autofocus steps at its own trigger points, so one session is a staircase against a continuous ramp and its slope partly encodes N.I.N.A.'s trigger policy; only the pooled fit estimates the physical quantity. Sessions with a small temperature span explode the ratio (one 2026-02-20 session gives 74.85 steps/C off 125 steps over 1.67 C, almost certainly a manual refocus or a disturbed train, and must be excluded rather than averaged in). And the fit needs `FOCUSPOS` to have actually **moved**, which is why SharpCap sessions contribute nothing to it (0 of 11 moved the focuser) even though they are what calibrates the *trigger threshold*.
