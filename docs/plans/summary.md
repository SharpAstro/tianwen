| [narrowband-colour](narrowband-colour.md) | **NOT STARTED** (researched + decided 2026-08-02). An Ha/OIII/SII stack has no colour path at all today: SPCC integrates a Pickles SED over the whole visible, which is the wrong model for a 3 nm passband. Research of three Siril techniques (which turned out to be three different things, not one) produced four ADRs. **ADR-1: ship robust statistical normalization first** (median offset then MAD/percentile gain applied *about the background*, plus an Ha/OIII palette lerp) because it needs no catalog, no plate solve and no new data, is built from primitives we already have, and is what actually fixes red-dominated HOO. It is explicitly **not** calibration and must not be labelled as such. **ADR-2:** the reference implementation (VeraLux Alchemy) is GPL-3.0 against our LGPL-2.1, so reimplement from the recorded maths and source the sensor crosstalk table from DBXtract rather than lifting it. **ADR-3: SPCC narrowband is BLOCKED and the original framing was wrong**: it cannot be done by pointing a narrow passband at Pickles SEDs, because a template is a spectral *type average* that cannot know whether a star shows Ha in absorption or emission over 3 nm; Siril uses Gaia DR3 `xp_sampled` per-star spectra, so this is a Gaia project. Also scoped to exclude SHO on Siril's own guidance (true SHO intensities are green-dominated; the Hubble palette is an artistic remap, not a calibration target). **ADR-4:** hue/chroma editing stays a *render* stage next to `MaskedBoost`, since a luminance range mask degenerates to zero on linear data. **ADR-5/6 came from a fourth video** ("RESCUE the BLUE", 2021, older than the rest): naive HOO is **rank-deficient**; `G` and `B` are the same array, so every OIII region is exactly cyan and no stretch can make blue. The fix is to mix ~15% Ha into blue standing for **H-beta**, which the Balmer decrement ties to Ha (intrinsic ratio 2.86, so Hb ~ 0.35 Ha; dust extinction is what drops the practical number to 15-20%). Our planned mixer already expresses this exactly, so it costs no mechanism, but the coefficients encode a line ratio and therefore ship as **named presets defaulting to a natural blend**, never bare sliders defaulting to naive HOO. ADR-6 records that P2 and P3 **conflict over blue** (P3's weighted OIII estimate discards the blue photosites' independent measurement that P2 would otherwise exploit), so P3 must not chain onto P2 blindly. **ADR-7/8 came from reading the fourth script** (`VeraLux_Curves.py`, initially deferred): it is the same role as AstroColorMixer but with **visible maths**, so it supersedes that investigation entirely, and it gives P5 a concrete shape (value-domain luminance masks, then `L`/`S`/`C`, with chroma as a hue-preserving scale of Lab `a`,`b`; four lines). ADR-7 records the opposite of the obvious conclusion: its Akima spline is **not** an upgrade over our `FritschCarlsonSpline`, which is monotone-*guaranteeing* where Akima merely reduces oscillation, and a non-monotone tone curve inverts local contrast. Do not "modernise" us to Akima. Our 33-knot LUT vs their 65536 is a deliberate std140 GPU-packing constraint, widened on both paths or neither. **The H-beta rationale turned out to be filter-dependent** (user, 2026-08-02), which amended ADR-5's default: an L-eNhance or quad-band *passes* Hb so its blue channel already contains it and adding Ha double-counts, while an Antlia 3nm or L-eXtreme *blocks* it so the blend invents a line that was never measured. On Hb-passing filters the "OIII" channel is literally OIII+Hb, which also means P3's two-line unmixing solves the wrong system there and P4 cannot even describe such a filter (one centre+bandwidth per channel cannot express a channel seeing two lines). Hue rotation has no filter dependency, so it is the only safe blind default; H-beta presets are gated on knowing the filter set, which is profile-level rig config. **ADR-9 then reframed the whole plan** (user, 2026-08-02): the primary goal is a richer picture, not a photometric measurement, and a science mode is additionally in scope, but the two are NOT in tension. One engine, modelled as precisely as the data allows in every mode; the mode gates what may be **added** (invented lines, aesthetic remaps), never how well we model, and anything synthetic is recorded as such in provenance. That inverted the Hb reading: an L-eNhance is not a *contaminated* OIII channel, it is a filter that captured a **third real line**, which solves the rank-2 problem physically (three lines across three channels is exactly determined, so blue is *recovered* rather than synthesised). The three-line solve is therefore promoted to P3a as the largest source of real colour, not deferred as pedantry; only the four-line quad-band case stays deferred (underdetermined). Prioritisation is by **signal recovered per unit of work**, which is why the purely statistical P1 leads (it fixes the actual complaint) and the most rigorous item, P4, sits at the back with almost no aesthetic payoff. **P0 continuum subtraction was found entirely unmodelled** (user, 2026-08-02; zero occurrences of "continuum" in the repo) and slots in ahead of everything: a 3nm filter passes a 3nm slice of *everything*, so an "Ha" frame is really `Ha + continuum` and every downstream statistic is fitted to that sum. Fix is `Ha - k*(R - median(R))` against a matched broadband frame, and the good way to get `k` is photometric: fit narrowband flux vs broadband flux over detected stars, **the slope IS k** since it is the ratio that cancels stellar images. **We already own most of that** (`FindStarsAsync`, quad-match registration, and the flux-fitting shape `Tycho2ColorCalibration` uses for SPCC), so the rigorous version is the cheap one here; the actual new work is pairing a narrowband `MasterGroupKey` with its broadband counterpart. ADR-10 keeps it OUT of the `IStarRemover` role: it removes stars *physically* rather than by inpainting, which is more honest, but it needs a broadband frame and leaves emission-line stars behind, so the two are not interchangeable. **ADR-11: masks may be sourced from a LINE IMAGE, and gate the colour-adjustment stage** (user screenshot of the 2021 video, 2026-08-02). The reference builds a `RangeSelection` mask **from the OIII line image itself** (observed: lower 0.24, fuzziness 0.00, smoothness 35.5, i.e. a hard threshold plus heavy spatial blur to get a structural envelope rather than a noisy per-pixel selection) and uses it to gate a **CurvesTransformation applied after a global mix** (target reads `Modified - Masked`; red pulled below the diagonal inside the mask, so OIII regions go blue while Ha regions keep their colour). So the shape is one global palette decision then one spatially-gated colour adjustment: P2 stays global, P5 gains the mask. The capability gap is that `LuminanceRangeMask` derives from *composite luminance*, which answers "is this bright" (bright Ha included), where the OIII channel answers "is there OIII here", which is the actual question. Strong pushes become safe because they only apply where the signal is: ADR-9 expressed as a mask instead of a model. The hand-tuned threshold is the one manual step and we can **automate it**: it is asking "is this above the noise", which `Image.Background()`'s clipped noise estimate already answers. Phases compound here too, since a threshold on a continuum-contaminated frame selects anything bright (stars included) while after P0 it is a true "is there OIII here" test. **ADR-12: the native input is a set of mono line planes** (`Ha`/`OIII`/`SII`/`Hb`), not an RGB image (user, 2026-08-02), which is how the reference actually works, its PixelMath operating on two grayscale windows `H` and `O`. Two hardware paths reach that state: **mono + filter wheel separates the lines OPTICALLY** (exact, no model, no crosstalk) while **OSC dual-band separates them ALGEBRAICALLY** (an estimate). So P3 is not a core phase, it is the OSC on-ramp, and a mono imager skips it and gets a strictly better result. Two things fall out: P1 restates as "align each weak plane to the reference plane" (Alchemy's "align G and B to R" is that with N fixed at 3), and **SII inverts**: it was never the hard part, four-from-three was; a mono imager with an SII filter just has a fourth plane and full SHO works with no unmixing at all. **Five transcripts + the Siril script repo (2026-08-02) grew this to SEVEN techniques and corrected two attributions.** The "Perfect Narrowband Colors" video is **NarrowbandNormalization** (Blanshan/Cranfield, ported by Cuiv), **not** SPCC, and it is the answer ADR-3 left missing for SHO: renormalize channels so Ha stops swamping green, instead of deleting green with SCNR. And AstroColorMixer's model is **not** locked in the app; the Siril port is public GPL-3.0, which superseded ADR-8: P5 now takes *selection* from ACM (hue-band masks, range masks, low-saturation/shadow/highlight protection ramps) and *adjustment* from VeraLux (Lab L/C, not ACM's HSL). ACM's band labels ("Red / H-alpha", "Cyan / OIII") reveal it uses **hue as a proxy for line identity** because after mixing that is all it has; we keep the line planes, so we select on ground truth. Also: `ContinuumSubtraction.py` gives a **better P0 scale solver than the star-photometry one**: minimise AAD of `nb - (co - median(co))*k`, coarse+fine sweep then an analytic smooth-V vertex fit, no star detection at all (correct subtraction = flattest residual). New **P6 narrowband star colour** (`NB_2_RGB`) fills a gap flagged twice: NB stars calibrate magenta, so synthesize them from the planes (`G = 0.3*Ha + 0.7*OIII`, the same 0.7/0.3 the RESCUE video used, independently). Nearly every phase now has a readable reference in `free-astro/siril-scripts`, including **DBXtract.py at its actual source**, which is what ADR-2 requires. **Validation vehicle is the `D:\Astro-Pics` dataset sweep** (user, 2026-08-02), which also surfaced a real blocker, **since fixed (2026-08-02)**: `SessionDiscovery` keyed sessions without the filter, so a mono Ha+OIII night became one session, the star-count gate rejected the OIII frames and the registrar stacked both filters into one meaningless master. Fixing it disproved two assumptions recorded here. Keying on the canonical `Filter.Name` (what `MasterGroupKey` compares on) would NOT have worked, because `Filter.FromName` is anchored and every descriptive header string (`Ha 3nm`, `Antlia ALP-T`) canonicalises to one `Filter.Unknown`; the key falls back to raw header text. And by the same route the `Bandpass` bit test below is **not** free on its own: `FILTCLAS` is a TianWen-written card, so a N.I.N.A. frame's bandpass comes from that same anchored parse and a 3 nm Ha frame reads `None`. **But the resolver is mostly already shipped** (user, 2026-08-03, correcting a draft that called for a new name parser without checking): `FilterCurveDatabase` embeds 176 spectral transmission curves with `TryMatchFilter` fuzzy name matching, so a bandpass is **measured** by interpolating throughput at 4861/5007/6563/6717 A rather than looked up. Coverage inverts the plan's assumption: the **OSC dual-band case is covered pre-convolved with the CFA** (`SONY_CMOS_{R,G,B}-UVIRCUT_/_<filter>` and Canon equivalents for L-eNhance, L-eXtreme, Antlia ALP-T, Antlia Triband), which is per-channel sensor-times-filter throughput, exactly what P3 unmixing needs and possibly enough to retire ADR-2's crosstalk-table question; **mono narrowband has zero curves**, so the widened name parse is still needed but only for the smaller half. Measuring the curves settled ADR-5 empirically (L-eNhance passes Hb at 64-67% via one wide 4820-5100 A band; L-eXtreme and ALP-T cut at 4960-4980 and block it) and **falsified the plan's `tri-band = Ha|Hb|OIII` claim**: Antlia Triband's three bands are blue 4260-4480 (Hgamma, not Hb), OIII, and a wide 6500-6820 red that passes SII at 74% continuously with Ha, so those two lines are not separable on it. A category name predicts nothing; derive the bits from the curve. The session key deliberately does **not** resolve through the database: grouping asks whether two frames share a filter, which raw text answers synchronously, and fuzzy-matching would let two different filters collapse into one session. An audit of the archive's distinct `FILTER` values is still the sweep's first output. Good news is how much already exists: `MasterGroupKey` carries `FilterName` + a **`Bandpass` bit-flags enum with Ha/Hb/OIII/SII members**, so narrowband dispatch needs no new metadata and ADR-5's does-this-filter-pass-Hb question is one bit test rather than a new profile field; and `LightGroupKey(MasterGroupKey, ObjectName)` is nearly P0's narrowband<->broadband pairing key already. Best verification prize: **measure our own crosstalk coefficients from the archive** rather than sourcing DBXtract's table, which retires the ADR-2 licence question and grounds P3 in this rig. **ADR-13 pins the mixing domain**: the reference stretches each plane then mixes, but once two planes have been through independent non-linear stretches, `0.7*O + 0.3*H` no longer mixes 70/30 of anything physical; we mix LINEAR (P1 does the make-commensurate job the per-plane stretch was doing, deliberately), so the published coefficients are starting points, not calibrated values. ## altaz-mount-support — Phase 0 DONE (Phases 1-3 NOT STARTED)

SkyWatcher mounts like the AZ-GTi are dual-mode (equatorial on a wedge / alt-azimuth flat).
The driver is equatorial-only; this plan covers making alt-az *safe* (shipped) then *usable*
(deferred). See [altaz-mount-support.md](altaz-mount-support.md) for the full gap analysis.

- **Phase 0 (safe — make an alt-az mount un-mis-pointable): DONE** (2026-06-22, PRs #47 + #48).
  - `?alignment=GermanPolar|Polar|AltAz` device setting (`SkywatcherDevice` / `DeviceQueryKey.Alignment`),
    default `GermanPolar`. AZ vs EQ is **not protocol-detectable** (same model code, `:q CanAzEq`
    only means "supports both"), so it's a user setting mirroring GSServer — never auto-detected.
  - `AltAz` is **report-only**: `GetAlignmentAsync` returns it and pier reads return `Unknown`, but
    `BeginSlewRaDecAsync` / `SyncRaDecAsync` / `SetTrackingAsync(true)` are **refused** via
    `SkywatcherMountDriverBase.EnsureEquatorialAlignment` (throws `NotSupportedException`).
    `MaybeSyncToPoleAfterSiteSetAsync` is skipped for alt-az (home is the raw encoder zero — az 0
    / alt 0, not the equatorial pole).
  - Meridian flip is now **GEM-only**: `ImagingLoopAsync` reads `AlignmentMode.GermanPolar` and
    fork/AltAz skip the whole flip cycle (PR #47). Pinned by the `Polar`/`AltAz` `[Theory]` in
    `SessionObservationLoopTests` (`MeridianFlipCount == 0`).
  - Tests: `FakeSkywatcherMountDriverTests` (`GivenAltAzAlignmentThenReportedButCoordinateOpsRefused`,
    `GivenNoAlignmentQueryThenDefaultsToGermanPolar`) + the GEM-only flip `[Theory]`.
- **Phase 1 (correct GOTO + tracking + position — visual/EAA/plate-solve, no imaging): NOT STARTED.**
  Az/Alt↔encoder-step transforms, `BeginSlewToTargetAsync` pier-gate bypass for alt-az (an
  `IMountDriver`-layer change that also unblocks ASCOM/Alpaca alt-az mounts), dual-axis predictor
  tracking, and Az/Alt→RA/Dec position reads. Tracked in TODO.md.
- **Phase 2 (alt-az guiding): NOT STARTED**, gated on Phase 1 (field rotates continuously, so the
  fixed-angle calibration assumption breaks; likely short unguided subs or a different guide model).
- **Phase 3 (long-exposure alt-az imaging): BLOCKED** on field-rotation handling — needs a mechanical
  derotator (`IRotatorDriver` / `DeviceType.Rotator`, which doesn't exist — see the `rotator` plan +
  TODO.md) or software derotation in the stacker.

**Recommendation (from the plan):** Phase 0 makes the alt-az case safe rather than silently wrong;
most AZ-GTi *imagers* run EQ-on-a-wedge anyway (already works as `GermanPolar`). Pursue Phase 1 only
on demand; defer Phase 3 until rotator support lands.
