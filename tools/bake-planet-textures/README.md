# bake-planet-textures

Bakes `src/TianWen.UI.Abstractions/Resources/planet-textures.png`, the sky atlas's OFFLINE planet colour maps
(docs/plans/atlas-planet-detail.md, "A4: the textured disc", #1037; the Earth since #1573, the Sun since #1608). One 512 x 640 PNG,
ten cells of 256 x 128, two to a row in `PlanetTextureAtlas.Layout` order (Mercury, Venus / Earth, Moon / Mars,
Jupiter / Saturn, Uranus / Neptune, Sun), each equirectangular in `PlanetMap`'s convention: planetographic latitude, row 0 the north edge, WEST
longitude with column 0 at 0 degrees and longitude falling to the right. The Sun's map is generated by the bake, not downloaded.

```
dotnet run --project tools/bake-planet-textures -c Release -- <download folder> src/TianWen.UI.Abstractions/Resources/planet-textures.png
```

Each source is downloaded into the folder unless already there, read in its own longitude convention (stated in
`Program.cs`, never shifted by hand), its holes filled where it has any (`PlanetColourMap.FromRgb8(fillHoles:
true)`), area-averaged to the cell, and written through `PlanetTextureAtlas.Encode`. The bake prints the file's
size and each body's mean colour: that is the review, since the PNG is binary. Re-bake by hand and commit.

Size on 2026-10-14: 301,799 bytes (eight bodies, 512 x 512). On 2026-10-16, with the Earth: 354,808 bytes
(512 x 640); the other eight cells are bit for bit the 2026-10-14 bake's. On 2026-10-16, with the Sun: 400,303 bytes; the
other nine cells are bit for bit the Earth bake's (compared pixel by pixel), and the bake prints the Sun's colour,
`1.0000 0.9449 0.9171`.

Solar System Scope answers .NET's `HttpClient` with a 403 (seen 2026-10-16) while `curl` gets the file, so fetch its
three maps into the download folder first, which the bake then reads instead of fetching:

```
for f in 2k_mercury.jpg 2k_venus_atmosphere.jpg 2k_mars.jpg; do curl -f -o <download folder>/$f https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/download/$f; done
```

### Why the Sun is generated

A real map of the Sun (SDO/HMI continuum, NASA, public domain, would be the source) is a picture of one day: its sunspots
would be drawn on every date. And at this size an observation says nothing a quiet photosphere does not: one sample spans
about 17,000 km of the equator by 17,000 km of latitude, some 170 granules of about 1,300 km, so the granulation's rms
contrast (of order 10 to 15 % at a granule's own scale, in the visible) averages to about a percent, and the
supergranulation does not show in the continuum. So the bake makes the map: the colour of a 5772 K black body (the IAU 2015
nominal effective temperature, Resolution B3) through the CIE 1931 2-degree colour matching functions (the multi-lobe fit
of Wyman, Sloan and Shirley, "Simple Analytic Approximations to the CIE XYZ Color Matching Functions", JCGT 2(2), 2013) into
sRGB, its brightest channel 1; times, per sample, 1 plus a Gaussian of 0.12 over the square root of the granules the sample
holds (fewer toward the pole, capped at three times the equator's spread), seeded so a re-bake writes the same file. The
limb darkening is not in the map: it belongs to the view, and the renderers apply it (`PlanetaryRender.LimbDarkening`).

The four OPAL maps are the same files the runtime upgrade fetches (`OpalColourMaps`), at the atlas's size, so the
offline texture and the upgrade differ only in resolution. Their URLs are in `OpalColourMaps.TryFor`.

OPAL's requested acknowledgement: "This work used data acquired from the NASA/ESA HST Space Telescope, associated
with OPAL program (PI: Simon, GO13937), and archived by the Space Telescope Science Institute, which is operated by
the Association of Universities for Research in Astronomy, Inc., under NASA contract NAS 5-26555. All maps are
available at http://dx.doi.org/10.17909/T9G593."

## Conventions checked

- OPAL: "left edge = 0 System III W. longitude, decreasing to the right" (the 2025 Jupiter readme), which is
  `PlanetMap`'s own; the Great Red Spot reads at west 321 degrees System III in the 2025b map, where its System II
  longitude at the map's date puts it (pinned by `PlanetTextureAtlasTests`).
- The Blue Marble is a plate carree centred on Greenwich with east to the right, the left edge 180 degrees, read as
  the other centred maps are: `PlanetTextureAtlasTests.TheEarthMapHasAfricaAtLongitudeZeroAndNorthUp` checks the
  Sahara at 22 N 30 E, the Atlantic at 22 N 30 W, Africa at 0 against the Pacific at 180, the sea at 22 S 8 E and
  Antarctica's ice, and that the same checks fail on the map read mirrored, flipped or half a turn round. Its deep
  sea is near black in the source itself (a tenth of the cell under the hole threshold), which is why the bake fills
  no holes in it.
- Solar System Scope and the SVS Moon Kit are centred on 0 degrees with east growing to the right: on Mars the
  darkest column at +8 degrees (Syrtis Major, 69 E) sits at 0.72 of the width, where (180 + 69) / 360 = 0.69.
- OPAL's composites are black where Hubble never saw the planet (Uranus's southern hemisphere in 2025, Saturn's north
  pole); the fill holds the nearest kept rows' mean colour there, never a row's longitude pattern.
